4.1¶

We start with the fact that:

\begin{align*} 1-p(X) &= 1-\frac{e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 X}}{1 + e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 X}}\\ &= \frac{1 + e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 X}}{1 + e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 X}}-\frac{e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 X}}{1 + e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 X}}\\ &= \frac{1}{1 + e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 X}}. \end{align*}

With this result we can easily get that,

\begin{align*} \frac{p(X)}{1-p(X)}=\frac{\frac{e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 X}}{1 + e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 X}}}{\frac{1}{1 + e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 X}}}=e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 X} \end{align*}

We first look at the expression from (4.17), first we want to separate the parts that depend on $k$ and the ones that do not,

\begin{align*} p_k(x) &= \underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma \displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{K} \pi_l \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma} \exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}(x-\mu_l)^2\right)}}_{k(x)\,>\,0} \cdot\, \pi_k \exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}(x-\mu_k)^2\right)\\ &=k(x)\pi_k \exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}(x^2+\mu_k^2-2x\mu_k)\right)\\ &=\underbrace{k(x)e^{-\frac{x^2}{2\sigma^2}}}_{c(x)>0}\pi_k \exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}(\mu_k^2-2x\mu_k)\right) \end{align*}

Here we can notice that for all $i\neq j$: \begin{align*} p_j(x) > p_i(x) &\Leftrightarrow\\ \pi_j \exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}(\mu_j^2-2x\mu_j)\right) > \pi_i \exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}(\mu_i^2-2x\mu_i)\right) &\Leftrightarrow\\ \log\left(\pi_j \exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}(\mu_j^2-2x\mu_j)\right) \right) > \log\left( \pi_i \exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}(\mu_i^2-2x\mu_i)\right)\right) &\Leftrightarrow \\ \delta_j(x) > \delta_i(x) \end{align*} where $\delta_k(x)=\log\pi_k+\frac{x\mu_k}{\sigma^2}-\frac{\mu_k^2}{2\sigma^2}$, which is the linear discriminant function (4.18) in the book. This holds since a positive constant and $\log$ preserve monotonicity.

4.3¶

The general idea or difference from before, is that the variance now depends on $k$ which means we cannot just collect the $x^2$ in the constant term. If we first look at $p$:

\begin{align*} p_k(x) &= \underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{K} \pi_l \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_l} \exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{2\sigma_l^2}(x-\mu_l)^2\right)}}_{k(x)\,>\,0} \cdot\, \frac{\pi_k}{\sigma_k} \exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{2\sigma_k^2}(x-\mu_k)^2\right) \end{align*}

Here $k(x)$ is constant across $k$. For all $i\neq j$: \begin{align*} p_j(x) > p_i(x) &\Leftrightarrow \\ \frac{\pi_j}{\sigma_j} \exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{2\sigma_j^2}(x-\mu_j)^2\right) > \frac{\pi_i}{\sigma_i} \exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{2\sigma_i^2}(x-\mu_i)^2\right) &\Leftrightarrow \\ \underbrace{\log\pi_j - \log\sigma_j - \dfrac{1}{2\sigma_j^2}(x-\mu_j)^2}_{\delta_j(x)} > \underbrace{\log\pi_i - \log\sigma_i - \dfrac{1}{2\sigma_i^2}(x-\mu_i)^2}_{\delta_i(x)} &\Leftrightarrow \\ \delta_j(x) > \delta_i(x) \end{align*} This holds since multiplying by the positive constant $k(x)$ and applying $\log$ both preserve the ordering.

\begin{align*} -\log\sigma_i - \frac{(x-\mu_i)^2}{2\sigma_i^2} &= -\log\sigma_j - \frac{(x-\mu_j)^2}{2\sigma_j^2} \\ &\Leftrightarrow\\ \frac{(x-\mu_i)^2}{\sigma_i^2} + \log\sigma_i^2 &= \frac{(x-\mu_j)^2}{\sigma_j^2} + \log\sigma_j^2 \\ &\Leftrightarrow\\ \underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_i^2}-\frac{1}{\sigma_j^2}\right)}_{a}x^2 - 2\underbrace{\left(\frac{\mu_i}{\sigma_i^2}-\frac{\mu_j}{\sigma_j^2}\right)}_{b}x + \underbrace{\frac{\mu_i^2}{\sigma_i^2}-\frac{\mu_j^2}{\sigma_j^2}+\log\frac{\sigma_i^2}{\sigma_j^2}}_{c} &= 0 \end{align*}

We end up with a quadratic equation.

5.1¶

Let's look at the variance as a function of $\alpha$

Let $g(\alpha) = \operatorname{Var}(\alpha X + (1-\alpha)Y)$.

\begin{align*} g(\alpha) &= \operatorname{Var}(\alpha X) + \operatorname{Var}((1-\alpha)Y) + 2\operatorname{Cov}(\alpha X,(1-\alpha)Y) \\ &= \alpha^2\sigma_X^2 + (1-\alpha)^2\sigma_Y^2 + 2\alpha(1-\alpha)\sigma_{XY} \\ &= \alpha^2\sigma_X^2 + (1-2\alpha+\alpha^2)\sigma_Y^2 + (2\alpha-2\alpha^2)\sigma_{XY} \\ &= \underbrace{\left(\sigma_X^2+\sigma_Y^2-2\sigma_{XY}\right)}_{A}\alpha^2 + \underbrace{2\left(\sigma_{XY}-\sigma_Y^2\right)}_{B}\alpha + \underbrace{\sigma_Y^2}_{C}. \end{align*}

$A = \sigma_X^2+\sigma_Y^2-2\sigma_{XY} = \operatorname{Var}(X-Y) > 0$, so $g$ is convex with a unique minimum. To find minimum, we set the derivative equal to $0$, and solve for $\alpha$:

\begin{align*} g'(\alpha) &= 2A\alpha + B = 0 \\ &\Leftrightarrow \alpha = -\frac{B}{2A} \\ &\Leftrightarrow \alpha = \frac{-2\left(\sigma_{XY}-\sigma_Y^2\right)}{2\left(\sigma_X^2+\sigma_Y^2-2\sigma_{XY}\right)} \\ &\Leftrightarrow \alpha = \frac{\sigma_Y^2-\sigma_{XY}}{\sigma_X^2+\sigma_Y^2-2\sigma_{XY}}. \end{align*}

Since $g''(\alpha) = 2A > 0$, this is the unique global minimum.

(a), (b), (c)¶

Let $X_1,...,X_n$ be the original data, with empirical distribution function,

\begin{align*} \hat{F}_n=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{I}(X_i \leq x) \end{align*}

We then draw a bootstrap sample, using the empirical distribution s.t. $X_1^*,...,X_n^* \sim \hat{F}_n$ they will all be $i.i.d.$ The probability of the j'th observation ending up in a single draw will then be $1/n$, so a single draw not being the j'th observation will have probability $1-1/n$. Since each draw in the bootstrap is independent, we can easily generalize this, first calculating the probability that the j'th observation will be in the bootstrap sample: \begin{align*} \mathbb{P}(X_j \notin \{X_1^*,...,X_n^*\}) &=\mathbb{P}(X_j \neq X_1^*,...,X_j \neq X_n^*)\\ &=\mathbb{P}(X_j \neq X_1^*)\cdots\mathbb{P}(X_j \neq X_n^*)\\ &=\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^n\\ & \Leftrightarrow\\ \mathbb{P}(X_j \in \{X_1^*,...,X_n^*\}) &=1-\mathbb{P}(X_j \notin \{X_1^*,...,X_n^*\})=1-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^n \end{align*}

(d)¶

For $n=5$: \begin{align*} \mathbb{P}(X_j \in \{X_1^*,...,X_5^*\}) = 1-\left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^5 = 1-0.32768 \approx 0.6723. \end{align*}

The general formula is used for $n\in\{5,100,10000\}$ in the plot below.

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

n = np.arange(1, 100_001)
p = 1 - (1 - 1 / n) ** n

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(9, 5))
ax.plot(n, p, color="#1f5fa8")

for n_val in [5, 100, 10_000]:
    p_val = 1 - (1 - 1 / n_val) ** n_val
    ax.axhline(p_val, color="gray", linestyle="--", linewidth=1)
    ax.plot(n_val, p_val, "o", color="#1f5fa8")
    ax.annotate(f"n = {n_val:,}: {p_val:.4f}", xy=(n_val, p_val),
                xytext=(8, 8), textcoords="offset points", fontsize=9)

ax.set_xscale("log")
ax.set_xlabel("Sample size n")
ax.set_ylabel("Probability")
ax.set_title("The j'th observation being in the bootstrap sample.")
ax.grid(alpha=0.3)
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
